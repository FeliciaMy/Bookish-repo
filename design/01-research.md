# Bookish — Research & Empathize

## Product essence

Bookish is a mobile-first community for people who love books: buy and sell pre-loved titles, and share reviews that spark conversation.

---

## Personas

### 1. Thandi — The Shelf-Clearer (Seller)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 28 |
| **Context** | Moves often; shelves overflow with finished reads |
| **Goal** | Pass books on to someone who’ll value them, and recover a bit of cash |
| **Tech comfort** | High — uses Instagram and marketplace apps |

**Jobs to be done**
- When I finish a book (or declutter), I want to list it quickly with photos and condition so buyers trust what they’re getting.
- When I set a price and handoff method, I want buyers nearby (or willing to use courier) to find me without endless DMs of “is this still available?”

**Pain points**
- General marketplaces bury books under unrelated clutter.
- Buyers ask the same questions (condition, shipping, payment) repeatedly.
- Feels awkward listing literary titles next to furniture and phones.

**Quote:** *“I just want someone who actually reads to take this home.”*

---

### 2. Lebo — The Bargain Browser (Buyer)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 24 |
| **Context** | Student / early career; loves physical books but watches budget |
| **Goal** | Find good-condition books near me (or shippable) at fair prices |
| **Tech comfort** | High |

**Jobs to be done**
- When I’m hunting a title or browsing casually, I want a feed of books for sale I can filter by condition, price, and location.
- When I find a listing, I want clear delivery and payment details so I know if a buy is realistic before I message.

**Pain points**
- Vague photos and “good condition” with no detail.
- Sellers who won’t ship or won’t meet in a convenient area.
- No way to ask a quick question on the listing itself.

**Quote:** *“Show me the spine, the price, and how I get it — then I’m in.”*

### 3. Sam — The Reviewer / Community Voice

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 32 |
| **Context** | Reads 2–3 books a month; already posts thoughts on socials |
| **Goal** | Share honest ratings and thoughts with people who care about books |
| **Tech comfort** | Medium–high |

**Jobs to be done**
- When I finish a book, I want to post a star rating, photos, and a short take without building an audience from scratch.
- When I see someone else’s review, I want to like it or leave a comment and feel part of a conversation.

**Pain points**
- Book talk gets lost in general social feeds.
- Review platforms feel clinical; social apps feel noisy.
- No natural bridge between “I loved this” and “someone nearby is selling it.”

**Quote:** *“I don’t need a blog — I need a bookshelf with friends.”*

---

## Shared insights

1. **Trust is visual** — Photos + condition language matter more than long descriptions for sales.
2. **Logistics kill deals** — Location, delivery, and payment must be upfront on every sale post.
3. **Two modes, one app** — Commerce and community should feel related but visually distinct (sale vs review).
4. **Mobile-first** — Listing and browsing happen on the phone, often between buses or on the couch.
5. **Low friction to contribute** — Creating a review or listing should feel like posting, not filling a tax form.

---

## Opportunity statements

- How might we help sellers list a pre-loved book with everything a buyer needs in one post?
- How might we help buyers discover and act on listings without leaving Bookish for unclear DMs?
- How might we give readers a warm place to review books and react to each other’s takes?
